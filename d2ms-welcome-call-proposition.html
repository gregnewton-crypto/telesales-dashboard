1 The Ask

Four things. None require new spend, new headcount or new systems — but the third and fourth need your explicit backing, and the first is a real call on my time that I have costed openly in section 11.

1. My time Two days a week, ring-fenced for calling: Tuesday = Marro, Friday = Butternut Box. Phase 1 starts with Marro only.
2. A Looker pull A scheduled Look listing the previous week's D2MS sign-ups with phone number, box dates and plan detail, delivered into Adversus.
3. An Adversus campaign A dedicated non-sales "Welcome Call" campaign with its own list IDs, result codes and recording, kept separate from acquisition dialling.
4. A holdout Permission to leave a randomised control group uncalled, so we can prove the effect rather than assert it.
Decision requested: approve a 12-week measured pilot on Marro, with a go / no-go gate at week 6 before Butternut Box is added.

2 Executive Summary

We spend roughly £90–£120 to acquire each D2MS customer, and then we never speak to them again. Fewer than half of Marro's D2MS customers make it to box 2. The reasons they give us are overwhelmingly things a five-minute conversation can fix.

The argument in five lines

  1. We are losing customers we have already paid for. Marro D2MS box 2 retention has drifted from ~51% in Q1 to ~44% in recent mature cohorts, while acquisition cost per sale has been running at £85–£125.
  2. The stated reasons are solvable, not structural. 41.7% of Marro pauses are "pet did not eat the food" — a transition and feeding-guidance problem, not a product rejection. Add price (20.5%) and "don't want a subscription" (23.3%) and 85.5% of all Marro pauses sit squarely in the territory a welcome call covers.
  3. Nobody currently owns the first three weeks. D2MS is paid on the sale. Once the sale lands, the customer is handed to automated comms only. There is no human check-in between sign-up and the box 2 payment — which is exactly where we lose them.
  4. The infrastructure already exists. The D2MS and Marro Looker pulls already carry name, phone number, order 1 and order 2 delivery dates, plan type, pause date and pause reason. Adversus already ingests list-based imports. This is a configuration job, not a build.
  5. It is cheap to test and honest to measure. A randomised holdout means we will know within one retention cycle whether this works. If it does, the prize is not the ~35 customers I call each week — it is what we learn and then automate across all ~450 weekly D2MS sign-ups.
The single most useful thing I expect to learn: whether "the pet won't eat it" is a transition-coaching failure or a genuine palatability problem. Those two answers lead to completely different business decisions, and right now we are guessing. Thirty-odd recorded conversations a week with real customers settles it.

4 Why Marro First

Marro is the right place to start for four reasons, and one of them is a finding I think we should act on regardless of whether this proposal is approved.

Finding: Marro's discount structure creates a price cliff that Butternut's does not. Across every week of 2026, Marro's box 1 discount is 40% and its box 2 discount is 30%. Butternut Box runs 30% on box 1 and 30% on box 2 — flat. That means a Marro customer's second bill is roughly 17% higher than their first, arriving in the same fortnight they are still deciding whether their cat likes the food. Butternut customers get no such shock. Marro's box 2 retention is 14.8pp lower and "price" accounts for 20.5% of its pauses. These facts are unlikely to be unrelated.

5 The Proposition

I personally call customers who signed up through D2MS in the previous week, as their named point of contact, to make sure they are happy, confident and correctly set up — and to answer the questions they never got to ask.

WHO

Last week's D2MS sign-ups

Customers whose subscription was created in the previous ISO week, on the D2MS channel, with a valid mobile number and no pause or cancellation already recorded.

WHEN

Tuesday = Marro, Friday = Butternut

Two ring-fenced calling days. Tuesday and Friday both sit in the sweet spot where box 1 has typically landed but the box 2 payment has not yet been taken.

WHAT

A service call, not a sales call

No upsell, no cross-sell, no target attached. The only objective is a confident, well-informed customer who knows how to run their subscription.

WHY ME

The person who can act on what I hear

I own the telesales performance picture. Hearing objections first-hand feeds directly into the D2MS scorecard, agent coaching, and the offer and onboarding decisions we are already debating.

6 Anatomy of the Call

Target six to eight minutes of talk time, structured in six blocks. Full wording is in the appendix.

BLOCK 1

Warm, named introduction

~45 seconds
  • Who I am and that this is not a sales call
  • Use the pet's name from the Looker record
  • Confirm I am speaking to the account holder
BLOCK 2

Did it arrive, and how is it going?

~90 seconds
  • Confirm box 1 arrived and was as expected
  • Open question on how the pet is taking to it
  • Listen for hesitation — this is the diagnostic moment
BLOCK 3

Feeding & transition coaching

~2 minutes
  • Gradual mixing over 7–10 days
  • Portioning, storage, defrosting, serving temperature
  • Explicitly normalise a fussy first week
  • Targets the single largest pause reason on both brands
BLOCK 4

App walkthrough, live on the call

~2 minutes
  • Confirm they have downloaded and logged in
  • Show where to change portion, frequency and delivery date
  • Show how to delay a box — the alternative to cancelling
  • Encourage adding the pet's profile and photo
BLOCK 5

Next box & the honest money conversation

~90 seconds
  • State the box 2 delivery date and charge date from the record
  • Marro: flag plainly that box 2 is at 30% off versus box 1 at 40%, so the bill will be a little higher
  • Offer to right-size the plan or lengthen the interval now, rather than lose them later
BLOCK 6

Questions, and a route back to a human

~60 seconds
  • Answer anything outstanding
  • Leave a clear contact route
  • Log outcome, risk flag and verbatim objection in Adversus
Why the box 2 price conversation is worth having deliberately. It feels counter-intuitive to raise price unprompted. But the customer will discover it anyway, at the worst possible moment, with no one to explain it. Telling them early, while offering a smaller plan as an alternative, converts a silent cancellation into a managed conversation. If the pilot shows this backfires, that is a genuinely useful finding and we stop doing it.

7 Operating Model

Looker builds the list, Adversus dials it, results come back into the reporting base. Weekly, repeatable, low-touch.

7.3 List selection rules

  • Include: subscription created in the previous ISO week; channel = D2MS; market = UK; valid mobile number present.
  • Exclude: already paused or cancelled; already contacted by Customer Care about a live issue; flagged as vulnerable; opted out of phone contact; duplicate phone numbers within the week.
  • Prioritise within the callable list, so that if I cannot get through everyone the highest-risk customers are dialled first (see 7.4).
  • Cap at three dial attempts per customer across the day, at varied times, then retire the record.

7.4 Prioritisation, since I cannot call everyone

With ~150 Marro and ~300 Butternut sign-ups a week and one person calling, I will reach a meaningful sample rather than the whole cohort. Rather than dial alphabetically, I would order the list by risk:

  1. No app login recorded — most likely to be confused and unsupported.
  2. No pet profile or photo added — weakly but consistently associated with lower retention in our own data.
  3. Largest plan or highest box 2 value — most exposed to the price step.
  4. Sold by an agent whose cohort retention is below the channel average — the sale may have been oversold.
  5. Everyone else, randomised.
Important caveat on prioritisation and measurement. Risk-ordering the dial list and running a clean randomised test pull in opposite directions. My recommendation is to randomise first (call group vs holdout), then risk-order within the call group only. That keeps the comparison valid while still making sure my dialling time goes where it matters most.

Partial coverage is a feature, not a bug. Because I can only reach a fraction of each cohort, the customers I do not reach form a natural comparison group. Combined with proper randomisation, that gives us a genuine read on effect size — something we would not get from a full-coverage rollout.

8.1 Sample size reality check

At ~34 conversations a week, a 12-week Marro pilot yields roughly 400 called customers against a similarly-sized randomised control. On a 48.6% baseline, that sample can detect a retention difference of very roughly 7 percentage points with reasonable confidence. Smaller true effects will show as a direction of travel rather than a statistically clean result.

I want to be straight about this up front. If the real effect is 2–3pp, a 12-week single-caller pilot will not prove it conclusively. That is an argument for judging this pilot on two things: the measured retention direction, and the qualitative insight from ~400 recorded customer conversations. The second deliverable is guaranteed; the first is probabilistic. I would ask that we agree that framing before we start, so success is not defined solely on a statistical threshold this sample cannot reach.

9.2 Adversus configuration

  • A dedicated campaign — "D2MS Welcome Calls" — entirely separate from acquisition dialling, so neither reporting nor dialling behaviour is contaminated.
  • Weekly list IDs in the form WELCOME-MARRO-W<nn> and WELCOME-BNB-W<nn>, matching the existing convention of tracking an Original List ID per import.
  • Preview dialling, not predictive. I need to read the record before I speak, and predictive dialling is inappropriate for a service call.
  • Custom result codes that record the outcome and the reason, not just the disposition — see 9.3.
  • Custom fields shown on-screen during the call: pet name, box 1 date, box 2 date, plan, discount code.
  • Call recording enabled, so the conversations can be used for D2MS coaching and the retention scorecard.
  • Outbound caller ID set to a recognised brand number with a working inbound route, so customers can call back.

9.4 Data-quality gaps I found while preparing this

Three issues worth fixing regardless of this proposal, because they currently limit our ability to see early-life churn at all:
  1. The app-launch flag in the Butternut D2MS Looker pull is unusable. It reads as true for every one of the 3,000 records I sampled, so it cannot currently distinguish an engaged customer from a disengaged one. Either the field or the underlying view needs correcting.
  2. Marro has no 84-day retention figure. The field is present in the weekly KPI table but is effectively empty for Marro, so we can only see Marro's box 2 step and are blind beyond it.
  3. Order 2 delivery date coverage in the Looker pull is patchy relative to the aggregated box 2 counts in the weekly KPI table, so the two sources disagree. Worth reconciling before we use the Look for anything operational.

10.3 Decision gates

WEEK 2

Operational viability

Is the Looker→Adversus flow working, and is the connect rate anywhere near 40%? If connect rates are very low, revisit call timing before spending more days on it.

WEEK 6

Go / no-go on Butternut

Early Marro cohorts have passed box 2. If the direction is positive and the conversations are yielding usable insight, add Friday Butternut calls. If not, stay on Marro or stop.

WEEK 12

Full read-out

Called versus control retention, objection themes, and a costed recommendation: stop, continue as-is, or scale.

WEEK 18

Mature cohort confirmation

Final cohorts fully matured past box 2, plus a first look at any 84-day effect.

What "scale" would mean if it works. Not me making more calls. It would mean either (a) folding welcome calls into the internal telesales team's week, (b) automating the specific fixes we discover — better transition guidance, clearer box 2 pricing, a nudged app walkthrough — across every sign-up rather than the fraction I can phone, or (c) building the call into the D2MS commission model so the agency has skin in the retention game. Option (b) is where the real money is.

11 Value Model

I have not baked in a retention uplift, because I do not know it yet — that is the point of the pilot. Instead, set the assumptions yourself and see what the pilot needs to achieve to be worth the time. Defaults use our actual baselines.

Assumptions

Let me be the first to point out the awkward number. On the default assumptions — two days a week valued at £250 a day, 34 conversations a day, £115 lifetime value — each conversation costs about £7.35, so I need to save one customer for roughly every 16 conversations just to break even. That is a 6.4pp retention uplift, which is a genuinely demanding target. On direct value alone, this pilot is somewhere between marginal and loss-making, and you would be right to challenge it on that basis.

I am not going to dress that up. The case for doing it does not rest on the direct return. It rests on the three things below.

Why it is still worth two days a week.
  1. The learnings scale; my dialling does not. Set the last input to even 1–2pp and look at the final tile. If these conversations tell us how to fix the food-transition message, the box 2 price expectation and the app walkthrough for every D2MS customer, a small uplift applied across ~450 weekly sign-ups dwarfs anything I can achieve on the phone myself. The calls are cheap primary research that happens to retain customers as a by-product.
  2. We currently have no qualitative view of early-life churn at all. We know 41.7% of Marro customers pause because the cat did not eat the food. We do not know whether that is a transition-coaching failure or genuine palatability — and the two answers point to completely different business decisions. Commissioning research to answer that would cost considerably more than my time.
  3. It de-risks a much bigger decision. If we ever want to fund a proper early-life retention team, or renegotiate D2MS commission around retained customers, we will need evidence that human contact in the first three weeks changes behaviour. This pilot buys that evidence at the lowest possible cost.
What would make the direct economics work. Three levers, in order of realism: raising conversations per day (a better connect rate is the single biggest factor — every extra conversation lowers cost per contact); targeting the calls at the highest-risk customers rather than the whole cohort, so the uplift per conversation is larger than the average; and eventually moving delivery to someone whose day costs less than mine. Try setting conversations per day to 45 and the cost to £150 to see the effect — which is essentially what a scaled, team-delivered version of this would look like.

11.1 The Marro-to-Butternut headroom, for scale

A useful upper bound: if Marro's box 2 retention matched Butternut's 63.4% rather than sitting at 48.6%, that 14.8pp difference on ~150 sign-ups a week would be roughly 22 additional customers reaching box 2 every week, or around 1,150 a year. I am not claiming welcome calls close that whole gap — some of it is brand, species and offer structure. But it does size the territory we are playing in, and it suggests the discount-cliff finding in section 4 deserves attention in its own right.

16 Appendix: Draft Call Guide

A guide, not a script — the tone should be a helpful human, not a read-out. Italics are indicative wording; the notes underneath explain what each part is for.

Block 1 — Introduction

"Hi, is that [name]? My name's Greg, I'm calling from [Marro / Butternut Box] — nothing to worry about and this isn't a sales call. You signed up with us last week for [pet name], and I just wanted to check in personally, make sure everything's arrived and gone smoothly, and answer anything you're not sure about. Have you got five minutes?"

Purpose: disarm the "why are they ringing me" reflex immediately, establish it is a service call, and use the pet's name to make it obviously personal.

Block 2 — Did it arrive, how is it going?

"So your first box should have arrived on [order 1 delivery date] — did that turn up alright? … And how's [pet name] getting on with it so far?"

Purpose: the most important listening moment on the call. Any hedging — "she's a bit unsure", "he's picking at it" — is the food-refusal pause reason forming in real time. Go straight to block 3.

Block 3 — Transition coaching

"That's completely normal, and it's actually the most common thing we hear in the first week — especially with cats, they can be pretty opinionated. The trick is not to switch them over all at once. Start with about a quarter of the new food mixed into what they're used to, and build it up over a week or so. A couple of other things that make a big difference: let it come up to room temperature rather than serving it straight from the fridge, and if they walk away from it, don't panic — just take it up and offer it again a bit later rather than putting something else down."

Purpose: directly targets 41.7% of Marro pauses and 33.3% of Butternut's. Normalising the fussy first week is as important as the practical advice, because it stops the customer concluding "my pet won't eat this" after two days.

Block 4 — App walkthrough

"Have you had a chance to get the app? … If you've got it to hand, I'll show you the two or three things worth knowing, because it's where you control everything. So if a box is coming at an awkward time, you can push it back — you don't have to cancel anything, you just delay it. Same with portion size and how often it comes. And while you're in there, it's worth adding [pet name]'s details and a photo, because that's what your portions get worked out from."

Purpose: "don't want a subscription" is 23.3% of Marro pauses and 15.9% of Butternut's. A lot of that is really "I don't want to be locked in". Showing them the delay button reframes the subscription as flexible. The photo prompt is a light engagement nudge that our data associates with slightly better retention.

Block 5 — Next box and the money

"Your next box is due [order 2 delivery date], and that'll be charged around [date]. One thing I want to flag so it's not a surprise — your first box had a bigger introductory discount on it than the ongoing one, so this next payment will be a bit higher than the first. It'll be [amount] rather than [amount]. If that's more than you were expecting, tell me now and we can look at a smaller plan or spacing the deliveries out a bit further — there's usually a way to make it work."

Purpose: the highest-risk and highest-value part of the call, and specific to Marro's 40%→30% structure. We are trading a small amount of short-term discomfort for the chance to keep the customer at a price that suits them, instead of losing them silently at the box 2 charge.

Block 6 — Close

"Is there anything else you're unsure about? … Great. You've got my name — if anything comes up with [pet name] or the deliveries, you can get us on [number] and there'll be a person on the end of it. Thanks for giving me five minutes, and I hope [pet name] gets on well with it."

Purpose: leave a human route back in. A customer who knows they can reach a person is less likely to resolve a problem by cancelling.
