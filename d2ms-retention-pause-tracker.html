D2MS Retention & Pause Tracker

A tracker design for early churn, rep attribution, product variety and pre-delivery pauses, built against the Marro D2MS V3 customer export.

Source: Marro D2MS V3 · 38,656 exported rows · 9,501 subscriptions · 9,160 customers · sales dated 2025-05-13 to 2026-08-03 · snapshot 2026-08-04

Read this first

The export cannot be measured as delivered. Before any of the five metrics will produce a trustworthy number, the file needs de-duplicating and one column needs quarantining.

The export fans every subscription out into roughly four near-identical rows. 38,656 rows describe only 9,501 subscriptions (9,160 customers). Every count taken straight from the raw sheet is inflated about 4×. The fan-out is a cross-product of Is Retained (2 values) × Interruption Reason (blank / populated), which is why 7,168 subscriptions appear exactly 4 times.
Is Retained (Yes / No) is an exploded pivot dimension, not a fact. Inside each fan-out group it splits 50/50 — a group of four always reads No, No, Yes, Yes. Across the whole file it returns 49.3% "Yes" regardless of what actually happened: customers with a second delivery split 2,508 Yes / 2,567 No, and customers with no second delivery split 297 Yes / 426 No. Counting this column will always return roughly half the file and tell you nothing. It must not be reported.
14 Day Retention and 28 Day Retention are trustworthy once collapsed. Unlike Is Retained, these never self-contradict: each subscription's group contains either blanks and 0.00%, or blanks and 100.00%, never both (0 contradictory groups out of 9,501). Take the single non-blank value per subscription and the field is sound — but it arrives as text, and blank means "not yet eligible", not "churned".

Baseline once the data is cleaned properly

The five requested metrics, measured on the de-duplicated table. These are the numbers the tracker should reproduce on day one, so they double as its acceptance test.

Early pauses, last full week 48 week of 27 Jul – 2 Aug, across 9 reps (prior week: 50)
Avg days to first pause 27.1 from sale; median just 9 days — report both
Avg distinct recipes 4.53 per subscription; only 1.6% take a single recipe
Pre-delivery pauses 391 4.1% of sales, plus 64 more that never got a delivery date
Early pause rate < day 7 24.2% 2,260 of 9,357 sales old enough to be at risk
The headline risk: 30.2% of all pauses happen inside the first six days, and 40% of every pause is pet_did_not_eat_food. Early churn is overwhelmingly a food-acceptance problem, not a pricing problem — price is only 21.4%. A tracker that only counts pauses will miss this; the pause-reason split is what makes it actionable.

Step 1 · Part B

Gap analysis: what is missing

Nine gaps stand between this export and the five requested metrics. The first three are blockers: no metric is safe until they are closed.

Severity Gap Why it breaks the metrics Fix
Blocker No unique subscription key, and rows are fanned out ~4× Every count, rate and average is inflated. "Total sales" reads 38,656 instead of 9,501, so the denominator of the early pause rate is wrong by 4×. Build Sub_Key from the user ID in Admin User Link + Animal Name + Subscription Created Date, then keep only the first occurrence.
Blocker Is Retained is an exploded dimension Any retention figure built on it is a coin flip dressed up as a metric, and it will look plausible (~49%). Quarantine the column. Derive retention from 28 Day Retention collapsed per subscription, or from the pause and delivery dates.
Blocker No maturity filter, so cohorts are right-censored A sale made yesterday cannot yet have paused within 7 days, but it still lands in the denominator and drags the rate down. 144 subscriptions in this file are younger than 7 days. Add Sub_Age_Days and Is_Mature, and restrict both numerator and denominator to mature subscriptions.
High No date-difference helper columns Days-to-pause, early-pause and pre-delivery flags cannot be filtered or averaged without them; nothing in the export measures elapsed time from a fixed point. Add Days_To_Pause, Days_Deliv_To_Pause, Pre_Delivery_Pause, Early_Pause.
High No rep master table Jagoda's results are split across two codes, so the league table understates her volume and misstates her rates. Nothing maps a code to a person, team or start date. Add a 08_Rep_Lookup tab: code → rep name, team, active flag, start date. Flag unmapped codes loudly.
High No dynamic "last week" logic, and no snapshot date "Last week" has to be re-typed at every refresh, and week-over-week comparisons drift. Days Since First Delivery already moves silently between exports. Pin Snapshot_Date once on a Control tab and derive every window from it.
Medium Retention fields are text percentages AVERAGE ignores text, so a retention average returns a silent 0 or an error rather than a wrong-looking number. Blank is ambiguous between "churned" and "too soon to tell". Convert to numeric on import and keep "not yet eligible" as a genuine blank, excluded from denominators.
Medium Subscription grain vs customer grain is undefined "Distinct recipes per customer" is ambiguous for the 267 customers with more than one pet, and they would be double-counted in per-customer averages. Step 2 · Part B

De-duplication: turning 38,656 rows into 9,501 subscriptions

Four helper columns on the raw import, then one query to build the clean base table. This is the step that makes every later number correct.

1. Helper columns appended to 02_Raw_Import

Paste the export into A:AP untouched, then add these to the right of it.

AR2 Sub_Key — the identity the export never provided =IF($W2="", "", REGEXEXTRACT($W2, "users/(\d+)") & "|" & LOWER(TRIM($M2)) & "|" & TEXT($C2, "yyyy-mm-dd")) AS2 Row_Seq — 1 on the first row of each fan-out group, 2+ on the copies =IF($AR2="", "", COUNTIF($AR$2:$AR2, $AR2)) AT2 Ret14_Num — text percentage to a real number, blank stays blank =IF($AK2="", "", IF(ISNUMBER($AK2), $AK2, IFERROR(VALUE($AK2), ""))) AU2 Ret28_Num — same treatment for the 28-day field =IF($AL2="", "", IF(ISNUMBER($AL2), $AL2, IFERROR(VALUE($AL2), "")))
Sub_Key includes the created date deliberately. There are no resubscriptions in the current file, but if a customer ever resubscribes the same pet, a key of user + pet alone would merge two genuine sales into one and quietly understate volume.

2. Build 03_Base — one row per subscription

A single formula in A2. The Col43 = 1 filter is what removes the fan-out. Tab names beginning with a digit must be single-quoted in Sheets formulas, as below.

'03_Base'!A2 — keeps only the first row of each fan-out group =QUERY( {'02_Raw_Import'!$B$2:$AP, '02_Raw_Import'!$AR$2:$AS}, "select Col42, Col1, Col2, Col3, Col4, Col8, Col9, Col12, Col21, Col39, Col41, Col35 where Col43 = 1", 0) Resulting columns: A Sub_Key B Rep_Code C Created D Pause E Pause_Reason F Deliv_1 G Deliv_2 H Animal I Trial_Days J Cohort_Week K Recipes L Recipe_Variety

3. Derived columns on 03_Base

These are the fields the export is missing entirely. Every metric in Step 2C reads from them, which keeps the dashboard formulas short enough to audit by eye.

Step 2 · Part C

The five metrics

Each metric is given as a Google Sheets formula against the named ranges above, with the SQL equivalent for when this moves off a spreadsheet. Every one reads from 03_Base, never from the raw import.

Metric 1 · Early pauses last week, by rep

On 05_Rep_Performance with the rep name in $A2. Counts pauses that happened last week and were early relative to their own sale date — two different conditions that are easy to conflate.

Early pauses last week for this rep =COUNTIFS(Rep_Name, $A2, Pause, ">="&Week_Start, Pause, "<="&Week_End, Early_Pause, 1) Same window, prior week — for the week-over-week arrow =COUNTIFS(Rep_Name, $A2, Pause, ">="&Prior_Week_Start, Pause, "<="&Prior_Week_End, Early_Pause, 1) All pauses last week, early or not — the context column =COUNTIFS(Rep_Name, $A2, Pause, ">="&Week_Start, Pause, "<="&Week_End) Team total for the summary tile =SUMIFS(Early_Pause, Pause, ">="&Week_Start, Pause, "<="&Week_End)
SQL equivalent SELECT rep_name, COUNT(*) AS pauses_last_week, COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE first_pause_date - created_date < 7) AS early_pauses FROM base_subscriptions WHERE first_pause_date BETWEEN :week_start AND :week_end GROUP BY rep_name ORDER BY early_pauses DESC;
Current reading (27 Jul – 2 Aug): 48 early pauses out of 164 total pauses, against 50 the week before. Franco leads on volume with 10, but Jennifer is the one to look at: 6 of her 7 pauses that week were early, versus 10 of Franco's 32.

Metric 2 · Average days to pause

Report the mean and the median together. The mean is 27.1 days and the median is 9 — a long tail of late pauses is hiding how fast the typical customer leaves.

From sale date — the headline =IFERROR(AVERAGEIFS(Days_To_Pause, Days_To_Pause, ">=0"), "n/a") Median, which is the more honest single number here =IFERROR(MEDIAN(FILTER(Days_To_Pause, Days_To_Pause<>"")), "n/a") From first delivery — includes negatives, so no filter =IFERROR(AVERAGE(FILTER(Days_Deliv_To_Pause, Days_Deliv_To_Pause<>"")), "n/a") Per rep, for the league table =IFERROR(AVERAGEIFS(Days_To_Pause, Rep_Name, $A2, Days_To_Pause, ">=0"), "n/a")
SQL equivalent SELECT AVG(first_pause_date - created_date) AS mean_days_to_pause, PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY first_pause_date - created_date) AS median_days, AVG(first_pause_date - order_1_delivery_date) AS mean_days_from_delivery FROM base_subscriptions WHERE first_pause_date IS NOT NULL;

Metric 3 · Recipe diversity

Distinct Recipes is already a count, so this is the one metric the export supports directly. The ">0" criterion excludes the 53 blanks rather than scoring them zero.

Per subscription — the headline figure =IFERROR(AVERAGEIF(Recipes, ">0"), "n/a") Per customer — excludes the 267 multi-pet households' extra pets =IFERROR(AVERAGEIFS(Recipes, Is_Primary_Pet, 1, Recipes, ">0"), "n/a") Per rep, to spot who is selling narrow baskets =IFERROR(AVERAGEIFS(Recipes, Rep_Name, $A2, Recipes, ">0"), "n/a") Share on a single recipe — the concentration risk =IFERROR(COUNTIF(Recipes, 1) / COUNTIF(Recipes, ">0"), "n/a")
SQL equivalent SELECT AVG(distinct_recipes) AS avg_recipes, AVG(distinct_recipes) FILTER (WHERE is_primary_pet) AS avg_recipes_per_customer, COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE distinct_recipes = 1)::numeric / COUNT(*) AS single_recipe_share FROM base_subscriptions WHERE distinct_recipes IS NOT NULL;

Metric 4 · Pre-delivery pauses

Report the companion count alongside it. 391 subscriptions paused before their first delivery, and a further 64 paused having never been given a delivery date at all — the strict Pause < Delivery test cannot see those.

Headline count =SUM(Pre_Delivery_Pause) As a share of all sales =IFERROR(SUM(Pre_Delivery_Pause) / COUNTA(Sub_Key), "n/a") The blind spot — paused, never had a delivery date =SUM(Paused_No_Delivery) Per rep =SUMIFS(Pre_Delivery_Pause, Rep_Name, $A2) Last week only, for the weekly pack =SUMIFS(Pre_Delivery_Pause, Pause, ">="&Week_Start, Pause, "<="&Week_End)
SQL equivalent SELECT COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE first_pause_date < order_1_delivery_date) AS pre_delivery_pauses, COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE first_pause_date IS NOT NULL AND order_1_delivery_date IS NULL) AS paused_never_delivered, ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE first_pause_date < order_1_delivery_date) / COUNT(*), 1) AS pre_delivery_pct FROM base_subscriptions;
Where the pre-delivery problem actually is. 60% of pre-delivery pauses say do_not_want_subscription (233 of 391) and 18% say i'm_coming_back (70) — against just 1.8% saying pet_did_not_eat_food. These customers are cancelling before they have tried anything, which points at the sale itself rather than the product. Christian's rate is 8.8% against a 4.1% average, and Braden's is 1.8% — a fivefold spread that a rep-level cut will surface immediately.

Metric 5 · Early pause rate (< day 7)

The one metric where the maturity filter genuinely changes the answer. Both numerator and denominator must be drawn from the same mature cohort.

Overall, with a small-sample guard =IF(COUNTIFS(Created, "<="&Maturity_Cutoff) < Min_Cohort_Size, "n/a", SUMIFS(Early_Pause, Created, "<="&Maturity_Cutoff) / COUNTIFS(Created, "<="&Maturity_Cutoff)) Per rep — same guard, so thin reps show n/a instead of noise =IF(COUNTIFS(Rep_Name, $A2, Created, "<="&Maturity_Cutoff) < Min_Cohort_Size, "n/a", SUMIFS(Early_Pause, Rep_Name, $A2, Created, "<="&Maturity_Cutoff) / COUNTIFS(Rep_Name, $A2, Created, "<="&Maturity_Cutoff)) Per cohort week — this drives the trend chart =IF(COUNTIFS(Cohort_Week, $A2, Created, "<="&Maturity_Cutoff) < Min_Cohort_Size, "n/a", SUMIFS(Early_Pause, Cohort_Week, $A2, Created, "<="&Maturity_Cutoff) / COUNTIFS(Cohort_Week, $A2, Created, "<="&Maturity_Cutoff))
SQL equivalent SELECT cohort_week, COUNT(*) AS mature_sales, COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE first_pause_date - created_date < 7) AS early_pauses, ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE first_pause_date - created_date < 7) / COUNT(*), 1) AS early_pause_rate FROM base_subscriptions WHERE created_date <= :snapshot_date - 7 -- maturity filter, both sides GROUP BY cohort_week HAVING COUNT(*) >= 30 -- small-sample suppression ORDER BY cohort_week;
The trap this filter avoids. Mixing a full-cohort numerator with a mature-only denominator makes the newest week of this file read 56.6% against a true 34.2%. Left unguarded, that lands as a fabricated crisis in the newest week, every single week — and it always points at whoever sold most recently.

Step 3 · Part B

Charts and visuals

Six visuals cover the brief. The first two answer "is it getting better or worse", the next two answer "who and why", and the last two are the ones that will actually change behaviour.

1. Early pause rate, week over week

Combo chart on 07_Weekly_Trend: columns for weekly sales volume, line on a secondary axis for early pause rate.

  • Plot cohort week on the x-axis, not pause week — you are judging the sale, not the calendar.
  • Leave suppressed weeks as genuine gaps. Do not plot a zero; a line dropping to the floor reads as success.
  • Add a target line. The average across the last 11 fully mature weeks is 27.8%.

2. Retention curve by cohort

Line chart, two series: 14-day and 28-day retention by cohort week, from the collapsed Ret_14 and Ret_28.

  • Current baseline: 45.1% at 14 days, 48.8% at 28 days.
  • 28-day sitting above 14-day is a censoring artefact, not a recovery — different cohorts are eligible for each. Footnote it or the chart will be misread.
  • Show the eligible count next to each point.

3. Pause reason mix over time

100% stacked column: reason share by pause week, on 06_Pause_Deep_Dive.

  • Group the long tail below 2% into "Other" — there are 12 raw reasons and half are noise.
  • Order the stack by size, with pet_did_not_eat_food at the base so the trend is readable.
  • The shift to watch: do_not_want_subscription is 21.5% of all pauses but 26.5% of early ones.

4. Rep scatter, rate against volume

Bubble chart: sales volume on x, early pause rate on y, bubble size = pre-delivery pauses.

  • Far better than a ranked list: it separates genuinely poor rates from small-sample noise visually.
  • Draw the team average as a crosshair to make four quadrants.
  • Christian lands high-volume and high-rate (736 sales, 32.5%); Jakub is the opposite corner (285 sales, 14.4%).

5. Days-to-pause histogram

Column chart over fixed buckets: day 0, 1–3, 4–6, 7–13, 14–27, 28–55, 56+.

  • This is the chart that justifies the whole programme: 30.2% of pauses land before day 7 and 57.7% before day 14.
  • Shade the pre-day-7 bars in the alert colour to anchor the definition visually.
  • Overlay last week's distribution as a line to see whether intervention is shifting the shape rightwards.

6. Recipe variety against retention

Combo chart: columns of subscription counts by Distinct Recipes (1–7), line for 28-day retention.

  • The signal is real: 7 recipes gives 56.4% retention and 19.3% early pause, against 46.7% and 25.1% at 4 recipes.
  • 4 recipes is the default-looking mode at 4,778 of 9,448 subscriptions — likely a preset rather than a choice.
  • Treat as correlation. Engaged customers may simply pick more recipes, so validate before setting a rep target.

Conditional formatting rules worth setting up

  • Rep league table: colour scale on early pause rate, green at 15% through red at 35%, so the eye goes to the outliers rather than the top of the list.
  • Suppressed cells: render n/a in grey italic. It must be obvious that a number is withheld rather than zero.
  • Unmapped rep codes: red fill on any Rep_Name starting UNMAPPED, so a new discount code is caught on the first refresh.
  • Snapshot staleness: amber banner on 04_Exec_Summary when Snapshot_Date is more than 8 days old.

A 7.5 point gap in early pause rate on samples of 5,562 and 3,793 — the largest single effect in the dataset. Note that 28-day retention is flat between the two, so the longer trial appears to delay the pause decision rather than prevent it. Worth an explicit test before anyone treats it as a lever, and worth a permanent cut in the tracker either way.

One caution on the rep numbers. Lead quality is not in this export. Reps do not necessarily work comparable lists, so these figures identify where to look, not who to blame. The 08_Rep_Lookup tab should carry team and start date so like can be compared with like.

Build order

Sequenced so that each step is verifiable before the next depends on it. Steps 1 to 4 are the ones that must not be skipped — everything after them is presentation.

  1. Stand up 01_Control and 08_Rep_Lookup. Map all 25 discount codes to 24 reps. Verify by checking that no Rep_Name reads UNMAPPED.
  2. Paste the export into 02_Raw_Import and add the four helper columns. Verify that Row_Seq reaches 1 exactly 9,501 times.
  3. Build 03_Base and its derived columns. Verify against this page's baseline: 9,501 rows, 4.53 average recipes, 391 pre-delivery pauses.
  4. Wire up 09_Data_Quality. Do this before any dashboard, so the first numbers anyone sees are already checked.
  5. Build 07_Weekly_Trend, then 04_Exec_Summary and 05_Rep_Performance on top of it. Reconcile the five tiles against the baseline before sharing.
  6. Add 06_Pause_Deep_Dive and 10_Action_List. The action list is what turns the tracker from a report into an intervention.
  7. Fix the export upstream. Everything above is a workaround for a fan-out that should not be in the file. A query returning one row per subscription, with a real subscription ID and numeric retention fields, removes steps 2 and 3 permanently and eliminates the largest standing risk to the numbers.
