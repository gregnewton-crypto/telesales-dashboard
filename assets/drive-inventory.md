## Content captured from the decks

### SPH

- SPH = sales divided by hours worked. Four shifts of seven hours is 28 hours.
  Worked example on the slide: 20 sales / 28 hours = 0.71.
- Targets: **0.6 is the standard**, **0.8 is good**, **1.0 is excellent**.
- IE has averaged 0.63.
- 0.6 over four shifts is 17 sales each, 51 as a team.
- 0.8 over four shifts is 23 sales each, 68 as a team. Holly has been consistently at this level.
- 1.0 is a sale every hour, 28 sales each per week.
- **The call structure, named in Part 1 and then taken one per deck:**
  Hook, Intro, Qualifying and Finding the Fix, Closing.
- Intro (Part 2): a buyer decides whether to stay on the line within 7 to 20
  seconds. 38% of the impression is voice and tone, only 7% is the words.
  Three elements: **Tone, Reason, Rapport**.
- Hook (Part 3): a statement, question or insight delivered straight after the
  greeting, addressing a pain point, trend or goal rather than pitching a
  feature. Three elements: **Reframe, Respond, Follow up**.
- Part 4 (Qualifying and Finding the Fix) and Part 5 (Closing) carry the correct
  section title but the body is duplicated from Part 2, including the heading
  "What make a good Hook".

### Objection handling

## The product is Butternut Box

The decks are about **Butternut Box**, a dog food subscription. The Demographics
deck says so directly ("Butternut Box looks great at a glance") and the personas
are all dog owners. SPH Part 2 ends with "Summarise what Butternut is".

This matters because the retention material already in this repository
(`transition-texture-training-deck.html`, `retention-improvement-master-plan.html`,
`d2ms-retention-pause-tracker.html`) is all **Marro**, a cat food subscription:
cat textures, recipes, fussy cats, the 65/90/130/180g portion bands and the
pause-reason mix. The dashboard covers both brands.

Until this is settled, treat any Marro figure in a lesson plan as needing a
Butternut equivalent before it is put in front of agents.
