The two days sit either side of each other on the clock: Day 1 runs the afternoon into the evening, Day 2 is a morning that finishes at 13:00. Together that is 9 hours of contact time.

The Shape of It

Day 1 - Who We Are Culture and structure, the product and why it beats the competition, who our customers are, and how the pitch is built.
Day 1 - Get Set Up Pay and logistics questions answered, then laptops and kit checked so nobody starts Day 2 blocked.
Day 2 - The Systems One focused hour, 09:00 to 10:00, on Adversus and how a rep moves through a call, then a 30-minute break.
Day 2 - Real Calls Three recorded calls (a sale, a not interested, a call back), a live call with Holly, then supervised practice in the test environment.

What Each Rep Should Be Able to Do by the End

  • Explain who we are, how the team is structured, and who to go to for what.
  • Describe our food and hold a confident comparison against the main competitors.
  • Talk through the pitch structure end to end and name our core customer types.
  • Understand their pay, how commission is earned, and when it is paid.
  • Log in and move through Adversus unaided: take a call, disposition it, and leave a clean note.
  • Recognise the difference between a good call and a weak call, and say why.
  • Have completed at least one full practice call in the test environment.

Worth a look before we lock this: systems is now one hour rather than a full morning. An hour is enough to get reps confidently taking a call and dispositioning it in Adversus, but order entry, payment and data-handling rules, and the wider tools will be a fast tour rather than hands-on. The practice session at 12:00 is where that gets consolidated, so it is workable, but if you want reps processing orders unaided by the end of Day 2 then order entry probably needs its own slot on a later day.

The Three Recorded Calls

Roughly 20 minutes each: listen, then unpack. Pick the calls in advance and listen to them yourself first, so the discussion points are already chosen and no time is lost hunting for the moment that mattered.

Call 1

The Successful Call

Listen for what to copy
  • How the rep opened and earned the time
  • The questions that uncovered the real need
  • How the product was matched to that need
  • How price was introduced
  • How the close was asked for
  • Whether the customer knew exactly what happens next
Call 2

The Not Interested

Listen for the turning point
  • Where the call was lost, and was it winnable
  • Was the objection real or just a brush-off
  • What the rep could have asked instead
  • How the rep left the door open
  • How to take a no without losing momentum
Call 3

The Call Back

Listen for the follow-up setup
  • Why the customer was not ready
  • Whether a firm time and reason were agreed
  • The quality of the note left behind
  • How it was dispositioned in Adversus
  • What makes a call back actually convert
Keep the debriefs consistent. For each call, ask the same three questions: what did the rep do well, what one change would have improved the outcome, and what would you do differently on your version of that call. Repeating the same frame is what makes it stick.
