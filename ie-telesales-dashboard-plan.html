Ireland Telesales Dashboard

Data audit of the IE Telesales Airtable base, and a page-by-page build specification with an exact definition for every chart.

0Executive summary

The IE base holds enough clean call and lead data to build a genuinely useful dashboard today, but it cannot reproduce the UK dashboard as-is. Roughly half of what the UK version shows is money and retention data that does not exist anywhere in the Ireland base.

7,472 leads, 99.6% of them dialled at least once
23,957 calls with agent, timestamp, duration and outcome
99.6% of calls join cleanly back to a lead record
8.7% of calls carry a loss reason — the biggest fixable gap
0 spend, budget, target or revenue records in the base
2 weeks of call data missing entirely (W13 and W32)

What I recommend we do

  1. Build eight pages, not six. Replicate the three UK pages the data supports (Overview, Leads, a reworked Staffing), drop the two it cannot support yet (Costs, Retention), and add four pages that the UK dashboard does not have but that this data strongly justifies — dialling cadence, call timing, live pipeline, and data health.
  2. Fix six data issues first. They are all small, and until they are fixed several headline numbers on the dashboard would be quietly wrong. Agent name spellings alone split one rep's activity across two rows.
  3. Add three small Airtable tables — targets, spend, and shift hours — to unlock the cost and productivity half of the UK dashboard. Nothing else is blocking.
The single most valuable thing this dashboard can show. The team currently places the largest share of its dials (15.2%) in the 18:00 hour, which is the worst-converting hour of the day at 1.43 sales per 100 dials. The best hour, 12:00, gets 12.4% of dials and returns 3.79. Separately, 1,927 leads (25.9%) were dialled repeatedly and never once answered, burning 6,510 dials — 27.3% of all dialling effort. Neither fact is visible in any tool today.

3Six things to fix before we build

These are not cosmetic. Each one would put a visibly wrong number on the dashboard if we built on top of the data as it stands.

3.1  Agent names are spelled several ways Blocking

Three of the eight agents appear under more than one spelling, so every per-agent metric is split across two rows and the top performer looks like two mediocre ones.

Fix: create the IE Agents table from §2, and map on it rather than on the raw Adversus string. Until that exists, the dashboard applies the alias map in tools/ie_deep_insights.py.

3.2  Two outcome fields that disagree Blocking

Adversus Lead Status (text) and Adversus Lead Status (single select) disagree on 547 leads, 7.3% of the base. They give different sales totals — 513 versus 583 — a 13.6% difference in the single most important number on the dashboard.

Fix: pick one as canonical, delete the other. I recommend the single-select, because it is constrained to eight valid values and matches the per-call Lead Status vocabulary. This needs a decision from whoever owns the Adversus sync before we build.

3.3  Two weeks of calls are missing Blocking

W13 has 504 leads and zero calls. W32 has 515 leads and zero calls. Both weeks recorded sales, so the calls certainly happened — they are absent from the export. W30 has nothing on either side.

Fix: re-run the Adversus export for 23–29 Mar and 3–9 Aug. Until then, the dashboard must render those weeks as an explicit gap rather than as a collapse in activity, or every activity chart will show two dramatic troughs that never happened.

3.4  Time between calls is not measuring time between calls Do not use

The field reports a median of 5 with 9,396 records sharing that exact value. Computing the real gap from the call timestamps gives a median of 2.0 days and a 90th percentile of 18.8 days. Sampled side by side, a record showing 5 has a true gap of 167.8 hours and one showing 10 has 187.2 hours. The values are unrelated to reality — most likely the dialler's configured redial delay rather than an observation.

Fix: exclude the field. The dashboard derives cadence from Session Start (No Offset) and Call # for Lead instead, which is verified correct.

3.5  The stale ⚙️ Call Week / ⚙️ Call Period select fields Important

These are filled on only 77% of calls, while the formula versions of the same fields are filled on 100%. They appear to be a manual snapshot that stopped being maintained around W27. Anything grouping on the select version silently loses 5,515 calls.

Fix: delete the select fields and use ⚙️ Call Week (formula) and ⚙️ Call Period (formula) everywhere.

3.6  An outcome is stamped on the lead's last dial, not the dial that earned it Design around it

115 of the 623 calls marked Success lasted 20 seconds or less, and the 10th percentile duration for a "successful" call is 1 second. A sale is not made in one second — the outcome flag is being written onto whichever call record was last touched.

Fix: for "which attempt won the sale", attribute to the lead's longest connected call rather than to the call flagged Success. The dashboard should state which rule it uses. Every attempt-level number in this document already uses the flag as-is, so treat attempt attribution as approximate until this is settled.

5.2  Colour palette — matches the UK dashboard

  • Coral #ED6961 — actual performance, the primary series
  • Blue #10507C — target or budget, always dashed
  • Amber #FFD54D — forecast or projection
  • Green #4CAF50 — volume and counts
  • Brown #522A10 — chrome, headers, tooltips
  • Grey #9E9E9E — immature, incomplete or below-sample data

5.3  Three conventions every chart must honour

1. Immature cohorts are visually distinct. A lead week is only 59.7% converted after 7 days and 90.2% after 28. Any week whose leads are less than 28 days old renders in grey hatching with the tooltip note "still maturing — expect this to rise". Without this, the last three weeks always look like a performance collapse.

2. Small samples are suppressed. Any bucket with fewer than 50 dials or fewer than 20 leads renders in grey and is excluded from ranking, with the note "sample too small". The 10:00 hour is the live example — it looks like the best hour on the board at 8.38 sales per 100 dials, but it has only 167 dials behind it.

3. Known data gaps are drawn as gaps. W13, W30 and W32 render as a hatched break in the line with the label "no call data exported", not as zero.

5.4  Refresh and access

Follow the UK build: a static page reading the Airtable REST API directly, token entered once and held in localStorage, auto-refresh every 5 minutes, hosted on Cloudflare Pages behind Cloudflare Access. At 7.5k leads and 24k calls the calls table is roughly 240 API pages, so the client should cache the last successful payload and show a "last updated" stamp rather than blocking on a cold load.

P3Call Effort & Cadence

Who it is for and what it answers

For the team lead setting dialler rules. Answers "how many times should we call, and how far apart?" — the single biggest lever on capacity, and invisible in every tool we have today.

A warning about the obvious version of this analysis. Grouping leads by their total attempt count suggests conversion collapses after two calls — 13.9% at one attempt, 14.4% at two, then 7.0%, 3.7%, 1.5%. That chart is wrong, and acting on it would cost real sales. A lead that converts stops being dialled, so converters pile up in the low-attempt buckets by construction. Every chart on this page uses the survivorship-corrected form: of the leads that actually received an Nth dial, what share converted on it. The corrected picture is far less dramatic and points to a different decision.

  1. Which outcome field is canonical? The two disagree on 7.3% of leads and give sales totals of 513 against 583. Whoever owns the Adversus sync needs to pick one.

  4. Who owns the four new tables? Spend, targets, shift log and agent roster. They are small, but they need an owner and a weekly update habit or they will be stale within a month.
  5. Should Marro be in scope? The IE base is Butternut only, while the UK dashboard covers both brands. If Marro Ireland exists or is planned, the brand filter should be built now rather than retrofitted.
