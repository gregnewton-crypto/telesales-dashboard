# Onboarding Deck Review

Review of the 78-slide telesales onboarding deck, checked against
`telesales-onboarding-schedule.html`. Slide numbers refer to the PDF export.

---

## What is already strong

Worth saying before the fixes, because the bones of this are good and the
structure is better than most sales inductions.

- **The "Why do people buy?" → "What are our benefits?" pairing** for dry, wet
  and raw is the best thing in the deck. It teaches reps to understand the
  customer's existing choice before countering it, which is exactly the habit
  that produces retained customers rather than pressured sign-ups.
- **The trigger-words exercises (slides 54 and 55)** are excellent. Giving reps
  real customer phrases and asking them to name the underlying need is the
  fastest way to build need-spotting, and it is active rather than passive.
- **The tasting (slide 24)** will do more for conviction than any slide. Keep it.
- **Two quiz recaps (slides 25 and 43)** break up the heaviest stretch.
- **Honest positioning.** Conceding that raw "is better than a lot of what is out
  there" and that we want to "champion dog owners for taking the step towards
  fresh" builds credibility. Reps who oversell get caught out; this framing
  protects them.
- **The customer-needs framework (slides 46-53)** maps needs to selling points,
  which is directly usable on a live call.

The sentence "The main ones you will come across are" stops there. If the logos

## 2. The pitch section does not exist

The Day 1 agenda (slide 2) lists seven items, and item **05 is "Pitching"**.
There are no pitching slides. The deck goes straight from the trigger-words
exercise (55) to "The Role" (56).

Searching the whole deck, the word "pitch" appears exactly twice in 78 slides:
once on the agenda, and once on slide 70 saying the first half of it is already
done. The word "objection" does not appear at all.

This matters more than anything else in this review, for two reasons:

1. The schedule gives the pitch a full hour, 16:00 to 17:00, inside the customers
   and pitch-work session. That hour currently has nothing behind it.
2. Day 2's slide 70 says "First half of the pitch down!", which only makes sense
   if the pitch has already been taught and rehearsed.

What is needed, based on the session outline in the schedule:

- The pitch structure stage by stage — open, discover, present, handle, close
- The discovery questions that surface the customer needs from slides 46-53
- The most common objections and the agreed responses
- One full pitch demonstrated end to end
- The pitch-work exercise itself: reps saying it out loud in pairs, taking
  objections, and getting feedback

There is also **no consolidated objection-handling slide** anywhere. The "Why do
people buy?" slides cover the reasons people chose their current food, which is
half the job, but a rep will hear "it's too expensive" on their first call and
there is no agreed answer in the deck. See the price point below.

## 3. No answer to the price objection

Slide 30 correctly lists "Cheap" as a reason people buy dry food. The dry
comparison on slide 31 then says nothing about price at all — while the wet
comparison (35) does say "Can often be similar price or cheaper".

Against dry food we are almost certainly more expensive, and dry is the biggest
part of the market. Reps need a straight, honest line for that: cost per day
rather than cost per box, what the customer stops buying, the health argument,
and permission to say we are not the cheapest. Right now the deck leaves them to
invent it on a recorded call.

should say which systems, and **"Soc"** is not decipherable to a new starter.

## 5. The pacing does not match the schedule

Counting slides against the time each session gets:

| Session | Slides | Count | Time | Per slide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome | 1-6 | 6 | 30 min | 5 min |
| Our Food + Other Foods | 7-43 | **37** | 90 min | **2.4 min** |
| Our Customers | 44-55 | 12 | 45 min | 3.75 min |
| Pitching | none | **0** | 60 min | — |
| The Role | 56-58 | 3 | 30 min | 10 min |
| Set Up | 59-60 | 2 | 30 min | hands-on |

The 90-minute food block also contains the tasting and both quiz recaps. Allow
ten minutes for the tasting and seven for each quiz and you are left with about
66 minutes for 34 content slides — under two minutes each, on the most
information-dense material in the two days.

Meanwhile the pitch hour has no slides and the Role's 30 minutes has three.

Two ways to fix it, and they are not exclusive:

- **Move the key-ingredients deep dive (slides 19-23) later.** Meat, lentils,
  superfoods and "free from" is genuinely useful detail, but it is reference
  material rather than something a rep needs before their first call. It would
  work well as a Day 2 slot or a follow-up session.
- **Build the pitch section**, which is needed anyway, and let it fill 16:00-17:00.

## 6. Claims to make safe

Several slides make health claims that a rep will repeat on a recorded call. None
of this is unusual for a product deck, but reps need to know which phrasing is
theirs to use and which is not.

| Slide | Claim | Concern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 22 | Turmeric "can assist in managing itchy skin, allergies, and hot spots"; Brewers Yeast as "Anxiety Supplement"; "Natural Anti-Inflammatory"; "Good for Dogs Joints" | Reads as a treatment claim for named conditions |
| 15 | "Good for pancreatitus & sensitive stomachs" | Naming a specific medical condition |
| 48 | "Several preservatives used have been proven to irritate dogs skin" | "Proven" needs a source |
| 53 | "Studies show fresh food extends dog lifespan"; "70% Dogs immune System in the gut" | Both need a citation reps can point to |
| 39 | "Recommended by Vets" versus raw's "Not usually recommended by vets" | Strong comparative claim; needs substantiation |
| 8 | "60% Grade A Quality Meat" | "Grade A" is not a recognised UK meat standard, so it invites a challenge |

Suggested addition: one slide, in the pitch section, headed something like "How to
say this safely" — a short list of phrasing swaps. For example "designed to be
gentle on sensitive stomachs" rather than "good for pancreatitis", and "lots of
our customers with itchy dogs tell us…" rather than asserting a clinical effect.
The schedule already allocates "claims we must never make" to the product
session, so there is a slot for it; there is just no slide.

Two facts worth double-checking before they are said to customers:

- **Slide 50:** "Oldest dog to switch to us was a 26 year old Jack Russell." 26 is
  extraordinary for a dog. If it is right it is a great story; if it is a
  mis-remembered 16, it is an easy credibility hit.
- **Slide 27:** "To call a food chicken, 4% of the recipe needs to be chicken."
  Worth confirming against current UK labelling rules, since it is a number reps
  will quote a lot.

## 7. Comparison slides: separate parity from advantage

On slide 39, two of the seven points in our column are things raw food also does:

- "No Preservatives or Chemicals" appears in **both** columns
- "Also Hypoallergenic" for us, "Hypoallergenic" for raw

A rep who uses those as differentiators against an informed raw feeder will get
corrected, and lose the room. Better to show them as parity — "same as raw" — and
let the real differentiators carry the argument: cooked so no bacterial risk,
fully balanced, portioned and delivered, superfoods.

Slide 31 has two smaller issues: a stray question mark in "It gets portioned and
delivered to the door?" and an orphaned "Convenient" at the bottom of the slide.

## 10. Smaller suggestions

- **Add slide numbers.** With 78 slides and three people presenting, "go back to
  47" is much easier than scrolling.
- **Put the presenter's name on each section divider.** Ed, Paddy, Holly and Greg
  each own different sections; the deck only names Ed, once, on slide 57.
- **The "Your Aim" slide (6) is the most important slide in the deck** — first two
  boxes, still active at 28 days. Consider repeating it as the closing slide
  instead of, or alongside, "WELL DONE", so the last thing reps see is the target
  rather than a congratulation.
- **Slide 18 is doing a lot of work** and is the slide reps will reach for most,
  since it is what the customer actually receives. Worth making it the cleanest
  slide in the deck.
- **Consider a one-page take-away** of the customer needs and their selling points
  (slides 46-53 condensed). That is the reference reps will want on the desk
  during their first shift, and it maps to the "pitch structure one-pager" in the
  prep checklist.
